# Vision

## The Idea Keep

**Ideas, experiments and things built below the surface.**

The Idea Keep is a personal publishing platform and public lab book for ideas, experiments, learnings, projects, and artifacts. It should make it easy to capture a thought, show how it develops, and share useful outcomes without requiring every entry to become a polished blog post.

The platform exists to make thinking and making visible: hypotheses can be tested, experiments can fail, projects can evolve, and learnings can be revisited. The process is part of the content.

## Principles

### Unfinished is publishable

An idea does not need to be complete to be worth sharing. Content can be published as a seed, a work in progress, a working theory, or a validated result. Its maturity should be clear to readers.

### Show the path, not only the outcome

Experiments, discarded approaches, and changes in understanding can be useful in their own right. The Keep should support links between an idea, the experiments it inspires, and the learnings or artifacts that result.

### Keep the structure understandable

Use clear content types and status labels. Dungeon-inspired names can add character, but the information architecture should remain legible to visitors who do not know the reference.

### Keep publishing lightweight

The initial platform should favor writing and sharing over building a complex publishing system. A simple, portable foundation leaves room to add capabilities when real needs emerge.

## Character and language

The name draws on the atmosphere of Dungeon Keeper: a place where ideas are hatched, experiments are explored, projects have a lair, and learnings are kept in a library. The theme should stay playful and subtle enough that the site remains approachable and useful on its own.

Possible section names include **The Hatchery** for ideas, **The Laboratory** for experiments, **The Lair** for projects, and **The Library** for learnings. These are working names, not a required navigation scheme.

## What success looks like

- New ideas can be captured and published before they are fully formed.
- Readers can distinguish an idea from an experiment, a learning, or a project, and understand how mature it is.
- Useful work can be found and followed as it changes over time.
- Maintaining the platform does not become a prerequisite for publishing on it.
