## Tales from Below

Now and then a raven drops through the shaft with news from the surface: a gate
breached in a distant fortress, a ledger stolen, a bridge that turned out to be
painted on the wall. Most of it echoes down the corridors for a day and fades.
Some of it stays with the Keeper. Those tidings get a table of their own: on
one side, what the ravens actually carried and where they picked it up; on the
other, what the Keeper makes of it down here. The two sides never share a
column, so anyone can see where the report ends and the reading begins.

## The idea

A separate wing for short, self-contained reports on events from outside the
Keep. Security incidents come first, but the wing is not limited to them. Each
tiding sets curated facts next to my own reading of them: what is known, and
what I conclude from it.

Some events keep me busy anyway. Writing down facts and conclusions separately
sharpens my own thinking and shows the reasoning, not just the verdict. That
fits the lab book idea, and the result may also be useful to others.

## Hypothesis

Short fact-versus-view write-ups on external incidents are worth publishing in a
lab book: they produce real learnings rather than reposts.

## Shape of the wing

- Tidings live in their own wing outside the chambers and do not consume chamber
  numbers. This egg represents the wing in the Hatchery.
- Each tiding has two mandatory parts in every revision: **Tidings**, the sober,
  sourced account with contradictions and open questions made explicit and an
  "as of" date; and **A View from Below**, my clearly marked interpretation. The
  view can be short but is never omitted.
- Curation is the evaluation: unusable sources do not appear. Doubts become
  visible only through concrete observations such as contradictions,
  retractions, or missing confirmation.
- Revisions are append-only. New facts arrive as a dated block with their own
  view; a changed view stays part of the record.

## Guardrail

No cadence. A tiding exists only when an event has seriously occupied me and I
have something of my own to say.

## Open question

Does the wing get a first real tiding, or does it wither before the first spade
hits the ground?
